Shopping List Privacy Policy

Last updated 31 October 2026 (scanning barcodes added)

Shopping List keeps your grocery list and helps you shop. It is designed to work without collecting personal data: there's no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and nothing you put in the app is sent to us.

Your list

Your items, notes, stores, aisles, the order you usually walk round each store, and the dates you bought things are saved on your iPhone only, in the app's own storage. They are never uploaded. The dates are used on your iPhone to suggest items you're probably due to buy again.

Like other app data, they're included in your iPhone's backups (in iCloud, if you back up there), which Apple handles. Deleting the app removes them from your iPhone.

Your location

Location is optional. If you allow it (While Using the App), Shopping List uses it to:

  • save where a store is when you tap its pin,
  • find the nearest of your saved stores, and
  • choose the store you're in when you open the app, and offer to add a shop you're in that isn't saved yet, both of which you can turn off with Choose the store I'm in in Settings.

This happens on your iPhone. Your location is not sent to us, and the app never tracks you in the background. To work out where you are, iOS uses Apple's location services; Apple's privacy policy applies to them. You can change location access at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.

Finding stores

If you use Find stores near me, the app asks Apple Maps for shops around you, sending your approximate location and anything you type in the search. When you open the app away from your saved stores, and location is allowed, it also asks Apple Maps for a supermarket where you're standing, to offer to add it. This goes to Apple, not to us, and Apple's privacy policy applies. The shops you add are saved on your iPhone like the rest of your list.

Scanning receipts

If you scan a receipt, the app uses the camera (with your permission) or a photo you choose. The text is read on your iPhone with Apple's built-in text recognition; the picture isn't uploaded or kept, and only the prices you save are stored, with the rest of your list.

Scanning barcodes

If you scan a product's barcode, the camera (or a photo you choose) is read on your iPhone. The barcode number is then sent to Open Food Facts, a free, open database of food products, to find the product's name and pack size. Only the barcode is sent, nothing about you, and a barcode you've scanned before is found on your iPhone without asking again. Open Food Facts' privacy policy applies to that request.

Widgets and Siri

So that widgets and the Live Activity can show your list, the app keeps a short copy of it (what's left to buy and your progress while shopping) in a folder on your iPhone shared only with its own widgets. If you use Siri to add items or hear your list, Apple handles what you say under Apple's privacy policy; the items themselves go only into the app on your iPhone.

Suggestions while you type

Suggestions come from your own items and from a list of common grocery names built into the app. Nothing you type leaves your iPhone.

Children

Shopping List isn't directed at children. It collects no personal data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date.
