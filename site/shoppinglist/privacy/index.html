Shopping List Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 November 2026 (sharing with family, the subscription, Apple Watch and Handoff, prices in your currency)

Shopping List keeps your grocery list and helps you shop. It is designed to work without collecting personal data: there's no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and nothing you put in the app is sent to us.

Your list

Your items, notes, stores, aisles, the order you usually walk round each store, and the dates you bought things are saved on your iPhone, in the app's own storage, along with your past shops, prices and settings. The dates are used on your iPhone to suggest items you're probably due to buy again.

If you're signed in to iCloud, the list is also kept in your own private iCloud storage so it's the same on your iPhone and iPad. Only your Apple Account can read it: we can't see it, and it isn't shared with anyone unless you share it (below). To stop this, turn off Shopping List in Settings › [your name] › iCloud; your list stays on your iPhone.

Like other app data, it's included in your iPhone's backups (in iCloud, if you back up there), which Apple handles. Deleting the app removes it from your iPhone; the copy in iCloud can be deleted in Settings › [your name] › iCloud.

Sharing with your family

If you choose Share the list in Settings, iOS's share sheet sends an iCloud invitation to the people you pick. Everyone who accepts can see and change the whole list: items, stores, aisles, past shops and settings. Their names, from their Apple Accounts, are shown in the app, and the items each person adds show their initials. This goes through your iCloud, not to us. Stop sharing or remove someone in Settings › Family › Manage sharing; someone who joined can leave there too, and their own list comes back as it was.

Your location

Location is optional. If you allow it (While Using the App), Shopping List uses it to:

  • save where a store is when you tap its pin,
  • find the nearest of your saved stores, and
  • choose the store you're in when you open the app, and offer to add a shop you're in that isn't saved yet, both of which you can turn off with Choose the store I'm in in Settings.

If you turn on Tell me when I arrive in Settings and allow location Always, iOS watches the places of your saved stores (not your route) and wakes the app when you reach one, so it can show a notification like "You're at Tesco: 6 things on your list". The app doesn't record where you go. Turn it off in Settings, or set location back to While Using the App, to stop it.

This happens on your iPhone. Your location is not sent to us, and the app never tracks your movements. To work out where you are, iOS uses Apple's location services; Apple's privacy policy applies to them. You can change location access at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.

Finding stores

If you use Find stores near me, the app asks Apple Maps for shops around you, sending your approximate location and anything you type in the search. When you open the app away from your saved stores, and location is allowed, it also asks Apple Maps for a supermarket where you're standing, to offer to add it. This goes to Apple, not to us, and Apple's privacy policy applies. The shops you add are saved on your iPhone like the rest of your list.

Scanning receipts

If you scan a receipt, the app uses the camera (with your permission) or a photo you choose. The text is read on your iPhone with Apple's built-in text recognition; the picture isn't uploaded or kept, and only the prices you save are stored, with the rest of your list.

Scanning barcodes

If you scan a product's barcode, the camera (or a photo you choose) is read on your iPhone. The barcode number is then sent to Open Food Facts, a free, open database of food products, to find the product's name and pack size; if it isn't a food, the same number is sent to its sister databases, Open Beauty Facts, Open Products Facts and Open Pet Food Facts. Only the barcode is sent, with the app's language so the name comes back in it, nothing about you, and a barcode you've scanned before is found on your iPhone without asking again. A photo of the pack is downloaded from their image server and kept on your iPhone.

Prices from other shoppers

When you open an item that has a barcode, the app asks Open Prices (run by Open Food Facts) for prices other shoppers have shared for it in your iPhone's currency, and shows a few. Only the barcode and the currency are sent. Nothing of yours is shared with Open Prices.

Open Food Facts' privacy policy applies to these requests.

Widgets, Apple Watch, Handoff and Siri

So that widgets and the Live Activity can show your list, the app keeps a short copy of it (what's left to buy and your progress while shopping) in a folder on your iPhone shared only with its own widgets. The Apple Watch app gets the same short copy from your iPhone, over Apple's connection between them, and sends back what you tick off. Handoff passes whether you're shopping, and at which store, to your own devices signed in to the same Apple Account, through Apple. If you use Siri to add items or hear your list, Apple handles what you say under Apple's privacy policy; the items themselves go only into the app on your iPhone.

Suggestions while you type

Suggestions come from your own items and from a list of common grocery names built into the app. Nothing you type leaves your iPhone.

Your subscription

Shopping List is a subscription, bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and your Apple Account; we never see your card details or who you are. The app asks the App Store only whether your Apple Account has a subscription (including one shared with you through Family Sharing), and remembers the answer on your iPhone. Manage or cancel it in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.

Children

Shopping List isn't directed at children. It collects no personal data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date.
